## 1. Tree Enumeration
### Theorem 4.15: Cayley's Tree Formula
**Statement:**
The number of distinct trees of order $n$ with a labeled vertex set is:
$$ n^{n-2} $$

### Proof (Double Counting Method)
Let $T_n$ be the number of trees on a labeled vertex set.
Let $\mathcal{T}$ be the set of sequences of the form $(v, e_1, \dots, e_n)$ where the edges $e_1, \dots, e_n$ form a **Directed Rooted Tree (DRT)** with root $v$.

We count $|\mathcal{T}|$ in two ways:

#### **Method 1: Direct Selection**
1.  Choose a labeled tree: $T_n$ ways.
2.  Choose a root vertex $v$: $n$ ways.
3.  Order the $n-1$ edges to build the sequence: $(n-1)!$ ways.

$$ |\mathcal{T}| = T_n \cdot n \cdot (n-1)! = T_n \cdot n! $$

#### **Method 2: Constructive Sequence**
View the $n$ vertices as $n$ trivial DRTs. We merge them step-by-step.
*   **Step 1:** Add a directed edge.
    *   1st vertex: Any of $n$ vertices.
    *   2nd vertex: Any of remaining $n-1$ vertices.
    *   Count $n_1 = n(n-1)$.
*   **Step $k$:** We start with $n-k+1$ DRTs. To add an edge:
    *   1st vertex: Can be any vertex ($n$ choices).
    *   2nd vertex: Must be a root of a *different* DRT. Since $k-1$ edges were already added, there are $n-(k-1)$ roots total. Excluding the root of the 1st vertex's component, there are $n-k$ valid targets.
    *   Count $n_k = n(n-k)$.

Total count is the product of all steps $k=1$ to $n-1$:
$$ |\mathcal{T}| = \prod_{k=1}^{n-1} n(n-k) = n^{n-1} (n-1)! $$

#### **Conclusion**
Equating the two counts:
$$ T_n \cdot n! = n^{n-1} (n-1)! $$
$$ T_n = \frac{n^{n-1} (n-1)!}{n!} = \frac{n^{n-1}}{n} $$
$$ \boxed{T_n = n^{n-2}} $$

## 2. Matrix Tree Theorem (Kirchhoff)
### Theorem 4.16
Let $G$ be a graph with vertex set $V(G) = \{v_1, \dots, v_n\}$ and adjacency matrix $A = [a_{ij}]$. Define the matrix $C = [c_{ij}]$ as:

*   $c_{ii} = \deg(v_i)$
*   $c_{ij} = -a_{ij}$ (i.e., $-1$ if adjacent, $0$ otherwise)

**Result:** The number of spanning trees of $G$ is equal to **any cofactor of $C$**.

> **Example Calculation ($K_4$):**
> For the complete graph on 4 vertices ($K_4$), every vertex has degree 3.
> $$ C = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 3 & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & 3 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} $$
> Taking the cofactor (determinant of a $3 \times 3$ minor):
> $$ \det \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 3 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = 16 $$
> Matches Cayley's formula: $4^{4-2} = 16$.

## 3. Bipartite Graphs
**Observation:** A graph is bipartite iff all of its components are bipartite. All nontrivial trees are bipartite.

### Theorem 1.12
A nontrivial graph $G$ is bipartite if and only if **$G$ has no odd cycles**.

### Proof
**Forward ($\Rightarrow$):**
Suppose $G$ is bipartite ($V = A \cup B$).
If $G$ contains an odd cycle $(v_1, v_2, \dots, v_{2k+1}, v_1)$:
*   $v_1 \in A \implies v_2 \in B \implies v_3 \in A \dots$
*   In general, odd-indexed vertices are in $A$, even-indexed in $B$.
*   The last vertex $v_{2k+1}$ is odd-indexed, so $v_{2k+1} \in A$.
*   The edge $v_{2k+1}v_1$ would connect two vertices in $A$, contradicting the bipartite definition. Thus, no odd cycles exist.

**Backward ($\Leftarrow$):**
Suppose $G$ has no odd cycles.
1.  Pick $u \in V(G)$.
2.  Define sets based on distance from $u$:
    *   $W = \{v \in V(G) : d(u,v) \text{ is odd} \}$
    *   $U = V(G) \setminus W$ (distance is even)
3.  **Claim:** $U$ and $W$ are independent sets.
    *   Assume for contradiction there is an edge $xy$ with $x, y \in W$.
    *   Then $d(u,x)$ is odd and $d(u,y)$ is odd.
    *   A closed walk is formed by: path $u \to x$ + edge $xy$ + path $y \to u$.
    *   Length = Odd + 1 + Odd = Odd number.
    *   This implies an odd cycle exists, contradicting the hypothesis.
4.  Therefore, no edges exist within $W$ or within $U$. $G$ is bipartite.

## 4. Existence of Regular Graphs
### Theorem 2.6
Let $n, r \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $0 \leq r \leq n-1$.
There exists an $r$-regular graph of order $n$ if and only if **at least one of $\{n, r\}$ is even** (so $nr$ is even).

### Proof (Harary Graph Construction)
We construct vertices $v_0, \dots, v_{n-1}$ in a circle.

**Case 1: $r$ is even**
Let $r = 2k$.
Connect each vertex $v_i$ to the $k$ closest vertices in both clockwise and counter-clockwise directions.
*   Edge set: $\{ v_i v_{i \pm \delta} : 1 \leq \delta \leq r/2 \}$
*   This creates a graph where every vertex has degree $r$.

**Case 2: $r$ is odd**
(This requires $n$ to be even).
1.  Construct the graph for $r-1$ (which is even) as in Case 1.
2.  Add **antipodal edges**: Connect each $v_i$ to the vertex exactly opposite it ($v_{i + n/2}$).
3.  This adds exactly 1 to the degree of every vertex.
4.  Total degree = $(r-1) + 1 = r$.
