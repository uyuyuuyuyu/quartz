## 1. Preliminary Results

### Theorem 5.18
Let $G$ be a $k$-connected graph and let $G'$ be a graph obtained from $G$ by adding a new vertex with degree $\ge k$. Then $G'$ is $k$-connected.
* **Proof:** Left as Homework (HW).

### Corollary 5.19 (The Fan Lemma)
If $G$ is a $k$-connected graph and $u, v_1, \dots, v_k$ are $k+1$ distinct vertices of $G$, then there exists a set of paths $\{P_i : i \in [k]\}$ such that:
1.  For all $i$, $P_i$ is a $u-v_i$ path.
2.  For distinct $i, j$, $V(P_i) \cap V(P_j) = \{u\}$ (The paths intersect only at the source).

> *Visual Interpretation:* This structure is often called a "fan". A single vertex $u$ sends out $k$ independent paths to $k$ specific targets.

## 2. Main Result: Cycles through $k$ Vertices

### Theorem 5.20 (Dirac)
If $G$ is a $k$-connected graph with $k \ge 2$, then **every $k$ vertices lie on a common cycle.**

#### Proof
**Strategy:** Induction on $k$.

**Base Case ($k=2$):**
* Let the vertices be $u, v$.
* By Menger's Theorem (or definition of 2-connected), there exist at least 2 internally disjoint paths between any pair of vertices.
* Together, these two paths form a cycle.

**Inductive Hypothesis:**
* Suppose the theorem holds for $(k-1)$-connected graphs ($k \ge 3$).
* Assume $G$ is $k$-connected.

**The Setup:**
1.  Let $S$ be a collection of $k$ vertices in $G$.
2.  Let $x \in S$.
3.  Since $G$ is $k$-connected, it is also $(k-1)$-connected.
4.  Consider the set $S \setminus \{x\}$, which has size $k-1$.
5.  By the Inductive Hypothesis, there exists a cycle $C$ in $G$ containing every vertex of $S \setminus \{x\}$.
6.  If $x$ is already on $C$, we are done. So, assume $x \notin V(C)$.

---

### 3. Construction Cases
We must now "splice" $x$ into the cycle $C$.

#### Case 1: $|C| = k-1$
The cycle $C$ contains *exactly* the vertices $S \setminus \{x\}$ and no others.

1.  Label the vertices on the cycle: $S \setminus \{x\} = \{s_1, \dots, s_{k-1}\}$.
2.  By **Corollary 5.19**, there exist $k-1$ internally disjoint paths from $x$ to $S \setminus \{x\}$.
3.  Let $s_1, s_2$ be adjacent vertices on $C$.
4.  Replace the edge $s_1s_2$ on the cycle with the path $s_1 \dots x \dots s_2$ obtained from the fan.
5.  **Result:** A larger cycle containing $V(C) \cup \{x\}$, which covers all of $S$.

#### Case 2: $|C| \ge k$
The cycle contains the required vertices plus "extra" vertices.

1.  Label the required vertices on the cycle: $S \setminus \{x\} = \{s_1, \dots, s_{k-1}\}$.
2.  These vertices divide the cycle $C$ into $k-1$ segments (intervals).
3.  Apply **Corollary 5.19**: There exist $k$ paths $P_1, \dots, P_k$ from $x$ to the cycle $C$.
    * Let $x_i$ be the first vertex on path $P_i$ that is also on $C$.
    * Let $P'_i$ be the portion of $P_i$ from $x$ to $x_i$.
    * Note: The paths $P'_i$ have no internal vertices from $C$, and they intersect only at $x$.

**The Pigeonhole Argument:**
1.  We have $k$ distinct landing points $\{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ on the cycle $C$.
2.  We have $k-1$ intervals created by the set $S \setminus \{x\}$.
3.  By the **Pigeonhole Principle**, at least two landing points, say $x_i$ and $x_j$, must fall into the *same interval*.
4.  This means the segment of $C$ between $x_i$ and $x_j$ contains **no** vertices from $S \setminus \{x\}$.

**Final Construction:**
* Delete the edges of $C$ strictly between $x_i$ and $x_j$ (the segment inside the interval).
* Replace that segment with the path $x_i \leftarrow P'_i \leftarrow x \rightarrow P'_j \rightarrow x_j$.
* **Result:** A new cycle that includes $x$ and maintains all vertices from $S \setminus \{x\}$.
