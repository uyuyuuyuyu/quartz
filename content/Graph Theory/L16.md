# Graph Theory: Hamiltonian Graphs and Closures



## 1. Theorem 6.5: Components and Subsets
* **Theorem**: If $G$ is a Hamiltonian graph, then for every proper subset $S$ of $V(G)$, the number of components $k(G-S)$ is less than or equal to $|S|$ ($k(G-S) \le |S|$).
* **Proof Outline**: 
    * Let $G$ be a Hamiltonian graph and let $S$ be any proper subset of $V(G)$.
    * Let the graph $G-S$ have multiple components, say $G_1, G_2$, etc..
    * Consider a Hamiltonian cycle $C$ of the graph $G$.
    * The proof tracks the vertices within the cycle $C$ as they transition between the subset $S$ and the components of $G-S$ to establish the inequality.

## 2. Corollary 6.7 (Gabriel Dirac's Theorem)
* **Corollary**: Let $G$ be a graph with $n$ vertices where $n \ge 3$. If for every pair of nonadjacent vertices $x$ and $y$, their degree sum satisfies a specific threshold (implied to be $d(x) + d(y) \ge n$), then $G$ is Hamiltonian.
* **Notes**: The lecture notes that the bounds for this corollary are the "best possible".

## 3. Theorem (Bondy-Chvátal)
* **Theorem**: Let $u$ and $v$ be distinct nonadjacent vertices in a graph $G$ of order $n$ such that their degree sum is $d(u) + d(v) \ge n$. Then, the graph $G$ is Hamiltonian if and only if $G+uv$ (the graph with the added edge $uv$) is Hamiltonian.
* **Proof Outline**:
    * **Forward direction**: If $G$ is Hamiltonian, then adding the edge to form $G+uv$ clearly means $G+uv$ is also Hamiltonian.
    * **Reverse direction**: Suppose $G+uv$ is Hamiltonian. This means $G$ must have a Hamiltonian path with endpoints $u$ and $v$. Suppose by way of contradiction (BWOC) that $G$ is non-Hamiltonian. Repeat the contradiction proof method used in Theorem 6.6 to show this is impossible.

## 4. Graph Closure and Theorem 6.9
* **Definition of Closure $C(G)$**: The closure of a graph $G$ is the graph obtained by continuously joining pairs of nonadjacent vertices whose degree sum (including the newly added edges) is at least $|G|$.
* **Theorem 6.9**: A graph is Hamiltonian if and only if its closure is Hamiltonian.
* **Observation (Uniqueness)**: The closure of a graph is unique.
* **Proof of Uniqueness Outline**: 
    * The proof structure is compared to the proof for Kruskal's minimum spanning tree algorithm. 
    * Suppose $G_1$ and $G_2$ are both closures of $G$. 
    * Assume $G_1$ was formed by adding edges $e_i$ in a certain order, and $G_2$ was formed by adding edges $f_i$. 
    * By supposing a contradiction (BWOC), you can analyze the degrees of the endpoints of the added edges in $G_1$ and $G_2$ to prove they must result in the exact same final graph.
