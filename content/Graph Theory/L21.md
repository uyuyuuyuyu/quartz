# §10.2 Vertex colouring

## Definitions
* A **proper $k$-colouring** of a graph $G(V,E)$ is an assignment $f: V \to [k]$ s.t. $uv \in E(G) \Rightarrow f(u) \neq f(v)$.
* A **colour class** of a colouring is $[a] = \{v \in V : f(v) = a\}$.
* $\chi(G)$ is the **chromatic number** of a graph $G$, the smallest $k$ s.t. the graph admits a proper $k$-colouring. 
* If a proper $k$-colouring of $G$ exists, we call $G$ **$k$-chromatic**.

## Observations & Intuition
* **Obs:** every colour class is an independent set.
    * *[Diagram: A graph with vertices labeled 1, 2, 3 showing a proper coloring]*
* We often think of edges as conflicts. Colour classes partition vertex set into conflict free sets.
* **eg.** let vtcs be classes that have a final exam; put an edge between classes sharing a student. Hopefully: find colour classes corresponding to exam times.
    * *[Diagram: Exam scheduling example grouping classes 443, 220, and 100 into a "Fri 2PM" time slot, and class 442 into a "Wed 1PM" time slot]*

## Bounds on Chromatic Number
Let $G$ be an $n$-vertex graph.

* **Obs:** $\chi(G) \le n$, with equality iff $G = K_n$.
    * *[Diagram: Example showing an independent set vs a complete graph]*
* **Obs:** $\chi(G) \le \Delta(G) + 1$
    * *Intuition:* Colour vtcs one-at-a-time, avoiding any colours already added to nhd [neighborhood] of current vtx.
    * *[Diagram: Small graph showing a vertex being colored based on its neighbors]*
* **Obs:** $\chi(G) \ge \frac{n}{\alpha(G)}$
    * $\alpha(G)$ is the size of the largest independent set.
    * *Intuition:* every colour appears on $\le \alpha(G)$ vtcs.
    * *[Diagram: Sets representing color classes bounded by the independence number]*

## What is a Chromatic Number?
The **chromatic number** of a graph, commonly denoted by the Greek letter $\chi(G)$, is the absolute minimum number of colors needed to "properly" color all the vertices in a graph. 

A proper vertex coloring means that no two adjacent vertices (vertices directly connected by an edge) are assigned the same color.

## Basic Chromatic Numbers
* $\chi(C_{2n}) = 2 = \Delta(G)$
* $\chi(C_{2n+1}) = 3 = \Delta(G) + 1$
* $\chi(K_n) = n = \Delta(G) + 1$
* $\chi(K_{a,b}) = 2$ *[Diagram: Bipartite graph showing sets of vertices]*
* $\chi(P_n) = \begin{cases} 2 & \text{if } n \ge 2 \\ 1 & \text{if } n = 1 \end{cases}$
* Any $r$-partite graph is $r$-colourable.

Recall: $\chi(G) \le \Delta(G) + 1$

## Brook's Thm (Thm 10.8)
$\forall$ connected graph $G$ that is not an odd cycle or a complete graph, $\chi(G) \le \Delta(G)$.

## Thm 10.9
Let $G$ be a graph.
$\chi(G) \le 1 + \max\{\delta(H) : H \subseteq G\}$.

Intuition: Better to start colouring with $u$ and end with $v$.
*[Diagram: A graph illustrating starting at an outer vertex u and moving inward to v]*

**Pf:**
Let $|G| = n$.
Let $G = G_n$, let $v_n$ be a vtx in $G_n$ of min degree.
Define $G_{n-1} = G_n - v_n$, let $v_{n-1}$ be a vtx in $G_{n-1}$ of min degree in $G_{n-1}$.
In general: let $v_i$ be a vtx of min degree in $G_i$, define $G_{i-1} = G_i - v_i$.
Then: $G_i = G[\{v_1, \dots, v_i\}]$ and $d_{G_i}(v_i) = \delta(G_i) \le \max\{\delta(H) : H \subseteq G\}$.
So if we colour in order from $v_1$ to $v_n$, we never have to avoid more than $d_{G_i}(v_i) \le \max\{\delta(H) : H \subseteq G\}$ colours. So, $G$ has a proper $[1 + \max\{\delta(H) : H \subseteq G\}]$ colouring.
*[Diagram: A sequence of graphs $G_7$ down to $G_1$, illustrating the sequential removal of minimum degree vertices]*

# Kempe Chains and Planar Graph Colouring

Given a proper colouring of a graph, we call a path whose vtcs are coloured with only two colours a Kempe chain.

**Lemma:**
Then the colouring 
$c'(u) = \begin{cases} c(u) & \text{if } u \notin V(K) \\ 3-c(u) & \text{if } u \in V(K) \end{cases}$ (toggles 1, 2)
is a proper colouring of $G$.

**Pf:** Let $uv \in E(G)$
If $u,v \notin V(K)$, then $c'(u) = c(u)$ and $c'(v) = c(v)$.
So $c(u) \neq c(v) \Rightarrow c'(u) \neq c'(v)$.
WLOG $u \in V(K)$, say WLOG $c(u) = 1$.
Note $c(v) \neq 1$. If $c(v) = 2$, then 
$v \in V(K)$, so $c'(v) = 1 \neq 2 = c'(u)$.
If $c(v) \neq 2$, then $v \notin V(K)$, so $c'(v) = c(v) \notin \{1,2\}$, so $c'(u) \neq c'(v)$.

## Thm 10.19 (Heawood, 1890)
Every planar graph is 5-colourable.

**Pf:**
Let $G$ be a planar graph. If $|G| \le 5$, then $\chi(G) \le 5$. Suppose $|G| \ge 6$ and all planar graphs of smaller order are 5-colourable.

If $\delta(G) \le 4$, let $v \in V(G)$ s.t. $d(v) \le 4$. By hypothesis, $\chi(G-v) \le 5$. Let $c$ be a proper 5-colouring of $G-v$. $\exists$ colour in $[5]$ that does not appear on $N(v)$. Extend $c$ to $G$ by assigning $c(v)$ such a colour.

So, assume $\delta(G) = 5$. Let $v \in V(G)$ s.t. $d(v) = 5$.
By hypothesis, $\exists$ proper colouring $c$ of $G-v$ with 5 colours. Fix a drawing of $G$. Let $N(v) = \{v_1, \dots, v_5\}$ in order, and
If $N(v)$ has $\le 4$ colours, assign a remaining colour to $v$, done.

*[Diagram: A star graph centered at $v$ with neighbors $v_1$ to $v_5$]*

Assume $N(v)$ uses all 5 colours, with $c(v_i) = i$ for $i \in \{1,2,3,4,5\}$.
Let $G_{1,3}$ be the subgraph of $G$ induced by $v$ and $[1] \cup [3]$.
If $v_1$ and $v_3$ are in different components of $G_{1,3}$, then by lemma, we can create a proper 5-colouring of $G-v$ s.t. $c'(v_1) = 3$, $\forall i > 1$, $c'(v_i) = i$. 
Then assigning $c'(v) = 1$ gives a proper 5-colouring of $G$. So, suppose $G_{1,3}$ does have a $v_1-v_3$ path. 

*[Diagram: A planar embedding showing a $v_1-v_3$ path that encloses $v_2$, making a $v_2-v_4$ path impossible without crossing]*

This path plus $v$ makes a cycle with $v_2$ in one region, $v_4$ in another.
Now consider $G_{2,4}$, the subgraph induced by $[2] \cup [4]$.
Since $G$ plane, $\nexists$ Kempe chain from $v_2$ to $v_4$ in $G_{2,4}$.
So we can (by lemma) a proper 5-colouring of $G-v$ s.t. $c'(v_2) = 4$, and $\forall i \neq 2$, $c'(v_i) = c(v_i) = i$.
Then assigning $v$ colour 2 results in a proper 5-colouring of $G$.

# Cliques, Triangle-Free Graphs, and Shadow Graphs

Recall: $\chi(G) \ge \omega(G)$
* $\omega(G)$ - clique #
* order of lgst complete subgraph

Bad intuition: high chromatic number implies high clique number.

Def: A graph is triangle free if it has no $K_3$-subgraph (ie clique number $\le 2$)

A shadow vtx $v'$ of a vtx $v$ in a graph $G$ is a new vtx added to $G$ s.t. $N(v') = N_G(v)$.
The shadow graph $S(G)$ is obtained from $G$ by adding a shadow vtx of every vtx of $G$.

**ex $S(C_5)$:**
*[Diagram: A $C_5$ pentagon drawn inside another $C_5$ pentagon, showing the shadow edges connecting the inner and outer vertices]*

The collection of shadow vtcs is an independent set.
No shadow vtx is adjacent to the vtx it is a shadow of.
