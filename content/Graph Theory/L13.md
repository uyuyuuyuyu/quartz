# Detailed Proof Mechanics & Additional Theorems

## 1. Menger's Theorem: Detailed Proof Cases
**Goal:** Prove by induction on $m = ||G||$ (number of edges).
**Base Case:** $k=1$ is trivial.

### The "Shortest Path" Argument (Crucial for Case 2)
In the proof for Menger's Theorem, a specific logical sequence is used regarding a shortest path $P$:
* **Setup:** Let $W$ be a minimum $u-v$ separating set.
* **Condition:** Suppose we are in **Case 2**, where $W$ is not fully contained in the intersection of neighbors. Specifically, $W \subseteq N(u) \setminus N(v)$.
* **Path Definition:** Let $P = uxy...v$ be a **shortest $u-v$ path**.
* **Key Deduction (Note 1):** The vertex $x$ must be in $W$.
    * *Reasoning:* Since $P$ is a shortest path, $u$ cannot be connected to any later vertex in the path (otherwise a shorter path would exist). Therefore, the *only* neighbor of $u$ in $P$ is $x$. Since $W$ disconnects $u$ and $v$, some vertex of $P$ must be in $W$. Thus, $x \in W$.

### The "Edge Removal" Logic ($G-e$)
* **Operation:** Consider removing edge $e = xy$.
* **Claim:** We claim that in $G-e$, the connectivity $\kappa_{G-e}(u,v) \ge k$ (or specifically handled by induction).
* **Separating Sets Z:**
    * Suppose there exists a separating set $Z$ in $G-e$ separating $u,v$.
    * **Crucial Step:** The sets $Z \cup \{x\}$ and $Z \cup \{y\}$ are separating sets in the original graph $G$.
    * Since $W$ was a minimum set, constraints on the size of $Z$ allow the induction to proceed .

## 2. Lemma: Connectivity of $G-e$ (Detailed Proof)
**Statement:** If $G$ is $k$-connected ($k \ge 2$) and $e$ is an edge, then $G-e$ is $(k-1)$-connected.

**Proof Mechanics:**
1.  **Contradiction Setup:** Suppose $G-e$ is *not* $(k-1)$-connected. Let $W$ be a vertex cut of $G-e$ with $|W| < k-1$.
2.  **Bridge Argument:** Since $W$ disconnects $G-e$ but not $G$, the edge $e$ must be a **bridge** in the graph $G-W$.
3.  **Endpoint Properties:** The endpoints of a bridge in $G-W$ must be **cut vertices** (unless they have degree 1).
    * **Case 1:** If endpoint $x$ has $\ge 2$ neighbors in $G-W$.
        * Then $W \cup \{x\}$ would be a cut in $G$.
        * This implies $|W| + 1 \ge k$ (since $G$ is $k$-connected).
        * Therefore, $|W| \ge k-1$ (Contradiction to assumption that $|W| < k-1$).
    * **Case 2:** If endpoint $x$ has only 1 neighbor in $G-W$.
        * This relates to the minimum degree $\delta(G)$.
        * We know $\kappa(G) \le \lambda(G) \le \delta(G)$. If $\delta(G) \ge k$, then $x$ must have sufficient degree, forcing $|W|$ to be larger.

## 4. Global Menger's Proof (Missing Detail)
**Handling the "Adjacent" Case ($u,v$ connected by edge $e$):**
When proving the global theorem for adjacent vertices $u, v$:
1.  We cannot use the standard Menger's theorem directly because $u$ and $v$ are adjacent (no separating set exists that doesn't include $u$ or $v$).
2.  **Workaround:** Apply the Lemma to $G-e$.
3.  Since $G$ is $k$-connected, $G-e$ is $(k-1)$-connected.
4.  By the Inductive Hypothesis (or $k-1$ case), there are $k-1$ disjoint paths in $G-e$.
5.  **Final Step:** The edge $e$ itself serves as the $k$-th path (length 1). Thus, total paths = $k$.
